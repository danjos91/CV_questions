Is this binary tree a mirror of itself?

You do not need prior tree theory. A binary tree is just circles connected downward: each circle may have a left child, a right child, both, or neither. “Mirror-symmetric” means: fold the drawing along the dashed line through the root, and every circle lands on a circle with the same number.

What a binary tree is (no theory required)

Think of a family photo hung on a wall. The person at the top is the root. Each person can hold at most two children’s hands: one on their left (drawn to the left on the page) and one on their right (drawn to the right). If a hand is empty, the input writes −1.

1. A node is just a circle

Vertex #2 with value 2 is a circle labeled 2. The number inside is the stored value. The small index (#2) is only an ID so we can say “left child is vertex 4”.

2. Left and right are on the page

“Left child of 2” means: draw a line from 2 down-left. “Right child of 3” means: draw a line from 3 down-right. It is not “left from the node’s point of view” — it is left/right as you look at the picture.

3. A mirror swaps left and right

Stand in front of a mirror. Your left hand appears on the right side of the reflection. Folding the tree does the same: the left arm of someone on the left lands on the right arm of their twin on the right.

How Example 1 is built from the input

Each line is one vertex: value   left-child-id   right-child-id. We start at vertex 1 (always the root) and hang children where the numbers point.

Step 1 · line 1

1 2 3
Root #1. Hang #2 on the left, #3 on the right.

Step 2 · line 2

2 4 −1
#2 has a left child #4, and no right child.

Step 3 · line 3

2 −1 5
#3 has no left child, and a right child #5.

Step 4 · leaves

3 −1 −1 twice. #4 and #5 are leaves — no further children.

Before folding — two arms, opposite sides

#4 hangs off the left of #2 (far left of the page). #5 hangs off the right of #3 (far right of the page). Empty inner slots (right of #2, left of #3) also face each other across the fold.

fold →

After folding — the left half flips onto the right

Folding is a left–right flip around the dashed line. The far-left circle (#4) must land on the far-right circle (#5). That is exactly left child of 2 ↔ right child of 3. Both hold value 3, so they match. YES.

Example 2 fails for the same reason: both #2 and #3 hang a child on the same side (both to the right). After the fold, a circle would try to land on an empty slot.

Why this works

  1. Push (left[1], right[1]).
  2. Pop a pair (a, b).
  3. If both are −1, skip. If one is missing or values differ, return NO.
  4. Otherwise push the outer pair (left[a], right[b]) and the inner pair (right[a], left[b]).
  5. Empty stack → YES. Iterative, because n can be 2·10⁵.

How to read the input

  1. Line 1 is n, the number of vertices.
  2. Line i+1 describes vertex i (1-based): value, left-child index, right-child index.
  3. Use −1 when a child does not exist. Root is always vertex 1.
